# Reading conventions (corpus and engine)

When a corpus line and the engine disagree, these rules decide which side is wrong. Each engine fix unit gets the lines of its theme and judges every one of them by these rules: engine wrong (fix the engine, add a unit test), corpus wrong (return a correction), or out of this unit's scope. A disagreement is never settled by changing a correct expectation to match the engine.

## Principles

1. **Never guess.** A reading comes only from explicit grammar. Where a text allows two readings, the engine gives candidates (alternatives or `Options`), never a silent choice. A later rule may rank candidates; it does not delete them.
2. **Classify a whole number span before reading its parts.** A run of digits, separators and attached units is first one of: money, version, score, quantity or age, duration, date, clock. Only a span that nothing more specific claims is read as a clock or a date, and its parts are never re-read as a second clock or date.
3. **Blockers are local.** A word or symbol directly before or after the span decides (currency words and symbols, version and release words, score words, age and size units, duration verbs and nouns), not a word anywhere in the sentence: "costs 4.50" is money, "starts at 4.50" is a clock; "took 15h30" is a duration, "starts at 15h30" is a clock.
4. **Dates stay dates.** `YYYY-MM-DD`, `YYYY.MM.DD`, `DD.MM.YYYY`, `D/M` or `M/D` by region keep working. A dotted triple without a four-digit year (`9.2.1`, `4.2.1`, `2.10.3`) is a version, never a date or a clock. A dotted day.month pair followed by an explicit clock is a date only, with no clock alternative ("am 03.10 um 15 Uhr", "20.11 o 18:00", "15.10 в 10:00" give `10-03 15:00`, `11-20 18:00`, `10-15 10:00`); a bare pair with no clock after it keeps both readings.
5. **A duration never becomes a clock.** "for 2 hours", "durera 2 heures", "3時間", "半小時", "90 phút", "trwał 2h", "took 15h30" are durations. A duration next to a clock or range of the same clause is that mention's `dN`; a duration with no clock is not a mention. A duration phrase later in the same sentence, introduced by a duration verb or noun ("进行两个小时", "持续三十分钟", "lasting 40 minutes", "durasi 2 jam", "com duração de 45 minutos"), belongs to that clock too.
6. **Relative times** ("in 90 minutes", "через полтора часа", "trong 1 giờ rưỡi", "1時間半後") are `+Nm` mentions.

## Notation conventions (the corpus follows the engine)

7. **Midnight.** A bare midnight word (midnight, Mitternacht, minuit, medianoche, meia-noite, mezzanotte, middernacht, północ, полночь, gece yarısı, nửa đêm, 午夜, 真夜中, 자정) is the coming midnight: `00:00+1`, on the named day when there is one (`w7 00:00+1` is the end of Sunday). "Before midnight" idioms are `~23:59`. So is "until the end of" a calendar date written with numbers or a month name ("đến hết ngày 30/6/2027", "until the end of 14 May", "jusqu'à la fin du 14 mai"): `~23:59` on that date. End-of-day idioms on a relative day or a weekday ("yarın gün sonuna kadar", "сегодня до конца дня", "EOD Friday") keep their end-of-workday clock as the corpus writes it. The end of a week, month or year is not a mention. A clock written with digits (`00:00`, `0時`) is `00:00`.
8. **Date headings.** A date followed by clocks in the same paragraph ("Party on Sep 12. Cake at 20:00.") merges into the first clock's mention; later clocks in that paragraph inherit it (`^`). A date that stands alone as its own line or paragraph (a heading, optionally with a weekday, a label such as 일정 or Termin, or a trailing colon) also merges into the first clock after it, even across blank lines, unless another date comes first; real announcements put the date on its own paragraph above "Godzina: 10.00". Either way the date is not reported as a separate date-only mention. A date with no clock after it stays a date-only mention.
8b. **Date inheritance.** Outside headings, a clock with no date of its own takes the date of the previous mention in the same paragraph and is marked `^` ("Standup tomorrow 9:30. Sync at 15:00 Singapore time." gives `+1d 09:30 …` and `+1d^ 15:00 …`). It never crosses a blank line, never overrides a date written for that clock, and a clock after a blank line has no date unless it writes one.
8c. **Next-day words relative to a mention.** 翌朝, 翌日, 次の日, the next morning, the following day, le lendemain and the like move the date one day after the previous mention's date: after `10-03 18:00–19:00 …`, "翌朝9時" is `10-04 09:00 …`. With no previous dated mention they mean tomorrow (`+1d`).
9. **Unresolved places.** `!X` only when a place clue points at X: a place preposition or case ending, a zone word ("X time"), or a target question. A capitalized unknown word without a clue gets no mark ("call with Tokiio at 11:00" has none). In Chinese and Japanese text, 在 is a place clue only as a word of its own, never inside 现在 / 現在, 正在, 在线 / 在線, 实在, 存在 and the like; the place after it is the longest known place name (在大阪参观 is Osaka), common location nouns (家, 门口, 楼下, 办公室) are not places, and an unknown run glued to the following words gets no mark.
9b. **Places mentioned in the sentence.** A place with a location clue ("in X", "w X", "X'de", "X에서"…) in the same sentence as a clock, but not attached to it, is a suggestion, written `~<IANA>` in the source slot ("The hotel in Buenos Aires confirms check-in at 14:00." → `- 14:00 ~America/Argentina/Buenos_Aires > -`). No suggestion for a counterpart ("with the X team", "mit X", "avec X"), a direction ("to X", "naar X", "nach X", "へ"), or when the sentence names two different places. A clue attached to the clock ("14:00 in Tokyo", "Tokyo time") stays a plain source. Ordinary capitalised nouns (all German nouns) are not place clues by themselves.
10. **Issue text.** `#invalidTime:…` and the other issues carry the offending text as written, without prepositions: `#invalidTime:25:61`, not `#invalidTime:um 25:61`.
11. **Targets.** `> X` is where the text asks to see the time. Only explicit wording sets it.
    - A question for the time somewhere ("What time is that in X?", "Wie spät ist das in X?", "¿Qué hora será en X?", "X では何時ですか", "X 몇 시예요?", "X'te saat kaç?", "X mấy giờ?") targets the mentions with a clock before it in the same paragraph, back to the previous such question. When its own sentence has a clock ("런던 오전 9시는 서울 몇 시예요?"), it targets that clock only. Mentions after the question get no target.
    - An arrow (`→`, `->`) from a written time to a place targets that mention ("15:00 KST → Sydney").
    - "My time" as the destination ("→ meine Zeit", "wat is dat in mijn tijd?", "제 시간으로는 몇 시", "nel mio fuso orario", "tính theo giờ của tôi", "benim saatimde") is the target `local` when the clock already has a source zone; on a clock with no source zone it is the source `local`.
    - The place of a target is neither the source nor a 9b suggestion. An unknown place in a target phrase gets `!X` (rule 9).
12. **Several days.** One clock, or none, shared by several days gives one mention per day, in text order. Each day carries the shared clock, end, zone and target. The days form one series, which the output marks so the host can show them as one row; days of a series never share an equivalence group. The corpus lists each day as its own mention ("Sprechstunde dienstags bis donnerstags 9–12 Uhr." → `w2 09:00–12:00 - > -`, `w3 …`, `w4 …`). Weekdays and relative days without a clock are not mentions, alone or in a list or range ("Office hours are Monday to Friday." gives nothing, like "See you Friday." or "à demain"); as a heading they still merge into the clock that follows (rule 8). Calendar dates without a clock stay date-only mentions (rule 8).
    - A list of days ("les 3 et 19 octobre", "agosto 4, 5, 6 y 7", "nos dias 12 e 13 de março", "dienstags und freitags", "火曜と金曜") gives each day.
    - A weekday range ("dienstags bis samstags", "Selasa sampai Kamis", "Tue–Sat", "火曜～土曜", "周二至周六") gives each weekday in it.
    - "Every <weekday> in <month>" ("every Wednesday in March 2027", "2027년 3월 매주 수요일", "2027年3月の毎週水曜") gives each date of that weekday in that month.
    - A date range ("3–9 April 2027", "12-14.03.2027", "del 2 de febrero al 15 de mayo de 2027", "từ ngày 3/6 đến ngày 10/6/2027") gives its start and its end. The start takes the month and year it does not write itself from the end. When the range has a daily clock range and spans at most 7 days ("du mardi 11 au jeudi 13 mars, de 23h à 5h"), it gives one mention per day instead; a clock range that crosses midnight starts on every day but the last.
